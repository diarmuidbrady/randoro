# User Needs

This file focuses on the need, meaning the problem a user is trying to solve, rather than the feature that might solve it. Features are logged separately in [TODO.md](TODO.md). Keeping the two apart makes it easier to find a better solution later without losing sight of the underlying problem.

One principle runs through most of what users say: keep it simple. A common user should be able to start a workout in a few clicks, and each need below is worth weighing against that.

## Core need (validated)
- An unpredictable interval timer for shadow boxing that is quick and intuitive to start. Users describe it as already intuitive and good as is, so this is worth protecting.

## Needs surfaced from users

- Training could reflect the rhythm of a real fight rather than staying flat. A round tends to have phases where you surge, settle, then surge again. A single constant intensity feels less like a real round.

- Users may want to practice against varied, realistic challenges. A southpaw feels different from an orthodox, and a tall rangy opponent different from a short pressure fighter. There is some interest in training that reflects who they might face rather than a generic average.

- Different sessions seem to call for different amounts of structure. Sometimes a user wants pure freedom with just a timer, sometimes only cues, sometimes active direction on what to work on. A single fixed mode does not cover every session.

- Some users lean toward coaching rather than only timing. Direction on what to do, before or during a round, could help those training alone without a coach present.

- During bag work the hands are occupied. With lace-up gloves on, a user cannot touch the phone mid-round, so anything that needs interaction during a round does not fit that context. Audio in and out matters more here.

- There is room to reduce the thinking needed to start. Users do not always want to configure a session from scratch, and a credible named starting point, such as a standard amateur or professional structure, could lower the barrier to beginning.

- Users seem to picture round difficulty in different ways. Some think in terms of how often something happens, others in terms of how many things happen. The same hard round is modelled differently by different people.

## Tension to watch
Each need above adds surface area. The most repeated signal from users is a wish to keep the app simple, so added depth is probably best kept optional and off the common path of opening the app and starting a workout.
